Is this your day?

You fell asleep. Three hours later you were wide awake. You lay there for another two hours. You finally dozed off. Then your alarm went off. Time to get up.

Coffee. You are not hungry, so that is breakfast. Two bites of a banana on the way out the door.

An hour of high intensity interval training. You go hard, because this fat has to go.

Home. Another coffee while you get dressed. Out the door.

Lunch at your desk, in a meeting. A salad with a piece of chicken breast on it.

By the afternoon you are exhausted. So you get another coffee, and a protein bar with it, because you have to get your protein in.

Home at 7:00. Another salad. Another piece of chicken breast.

That is two salads, a protein bar and two bites of a banana. On a day you trained hard for an hour.

And you cannot work out why you are gaining fat.

So why are you gaining fat?

Are you eating enough?

You think you are. But who told you that was the right amount? How much are you actually eating in a day?

You are eating clean. You are not losing fat. So something else is off.

How are you sleeping?

Are you sleeping through the night? Are you getting at least 7 hours? Do you wake up rested?

If the answer is no, you are not going to lose fat. Your body needs sleep to burn fat.

Are you training harder?

The way you were training stopped working. So you did the obvious thing. You went harder.

Longer sessions. More intense. 5 or 6 days a week, 90 minutes to 2 hours.

And you are gaining fat.

It is not about training harder. It is about training smart, and getting your body into a state where it can lose fat.

Is your body even ready to lose fat?

This is the one nobody asks you.

You can eat clean. You can eat enough. You can train hard. But if your body does not feel safe, it will hold onto fat instead of burning it.

Here is how you know your body is not ready yet:

  • You cannot fall asleep, or you cannot stay asleep.
  • You are bloated. You are constipated. Your stomach feels inflamed.
  • You are having hot flashes. Night sweats. Your periods are all over the place. Your head is foggy.

If that is you, your body is not ready. And until it is, eating less and training harder will keep putting fat on you.

Your body is not the body you had at 30.

Losing fat in perimenopause takes a different approach than it did in your 20s and 30s. You already know that. You are doing exactly what you have always done, and you are gaining.

How the 3 months work

Month 1

We get your body ready.

We get you sleeping through the night and waking up with energy.

We sort out the bloating, the constipation, the inflammation.

We look at what is going on with your hormones.

And we go through your days. What you eat. When you eat. How you sleep. How you train.

Nothing in this month is about eating less.

Month 2

Your body is ready, and the scale starts moving.

The bloat is gone. Your energy holds all day instead of disappearing in the afternoon.

You are eating more than you were. And the scale is going down.

That is the water, the bloat and the inflammation coming off you. It is real weight, and you will feel it in your clothes before you see it on the scale.

Month 3

Now we take the fat off.

Your body can finally do what it could not do in January.

You eat the amount your body needs to lose fat. Your number, not an app's number.
